Every curve in the ICMNS logo is computed, not drawn: the folds of the cortex are a solution of a pattern-forming equation, the cerebellum is the phase plane of a model neuron, and the brainstem is an integral sign. The logo is generated by a short Python program, and the same program, run with the same options, always produces the same picture. The [source code](https://github.com/icmns-org/icmns-logo) is public.

## The cortex: a Turing pattern

The folds (gyri and sulci) are a labyrinthine Turing pattern, a solution of the **Swift–Hohenberg equation**

$$ \partial_t u = r\,u - \bigl(1 + k_0^{-2}\nabla^2\bigr)^2 u - u^3 , $$

introduced by Jack Swift and Pierre Hohenberg in 1977 to describe convection rolls, and now a standard model of pattern formation. For $$r > 0$$ the uniform state is unstable to perturbations with wavenumber close to $$k_0$$, and stripes of wavelength $$2\pi/k_0$$ emerge and lock into a maze.

The logo uses $$r = 0.3$$ and a wavelength of 0.19 (in units where the hemisphere is about 2.3 long). Starting from random noise on a 640 × 480 grid, the equation is integrated for 170 steps of size 0.5 with a semi-implicit Fourier spectral scheme: the linear operator is treated exactly in Fourier space and the cubic term explicitly. In the last 60 steps the solution is pinned to a negative value just outside the brain, which rounds off the folds at the edge of the cortex.

The fibres are three **level sets** of the solution, $$u = -0.12$$, $$0.18$$ and $$0.45$$, so each fold is drawn as a bundle of nested curves.

## The colours: tractography

Each short piece of fibre is coloured by its direction, borrowing the idea of the colour code of diffusion tensor imaging (DTI), which maps the orientation of white-matter tracts in the living brain: one colour for front-to-back fibres, one for top-to-bottom, and two for the oblique directions. In the logo the four directions take colours from the Nord palette (below).

## The outline

The outline of the hemisphere is a closed **Catmull–Rom spline** through 22 points traced from a lateral view of a real left hemisphere: frontal lobe, parietal arch, occipital pole, temporal lobe and pole. The program turns it into a **signed distance function**, negative inside and positive outside, which both confines the pattern and draws the two outer contours. The lateral (Sylvian) fissure is cut in with a smooth maximum.

## The cerebellum: a spiking neuron

The cerebellum, a closed spline in proportion to the cerebrum and tucked under the occipital lobe, is filled with the phase plane of the **FitzHugh–Nagumo model**, the classic two-variable model of a spiking neuron, due to Richard FitzHugh (1961) and Jin-ichi Nagumo and colleagues (1962):

$$ \dot v = v - \tfrac{1}{3} v^3 - w + I, \qquad \dot w = \varepsilon\,(v + a - b\,w). $$

Here $$v$$ plays the role of the membrane potential and $$w$$ of a slow recovery variable. With $$a = 0.7$$, $$b = 0.8$$, $$I = 0.5$$ and $$\varepsilon = 0.2$$, the rest state is an unstable focus surrounded by a stable **limit cycle**: the neuron fires periodically. The bold closed curve is that limit cycle, computed by integrating the equations until the trajectory settles and keeping exactly one period. (The textbook value is $$\varepsilon = 0.08$$; the larger value keeps the same dynamics but makes the rotation of the flow visible at the size of a logo.)

The thinner lines are **streamlines** of the vector field: starting points on a jittered grid, each traced a fixed length forwards and backwards along the direction of the flow, and kept only if they do not crowd a line already drawn. Together they show every trajectory spiralling out of the rest state or in from outside, onto the cycle. They are coloured by direction, like the fibres of the cortex.

## The brainstem: an integral sign

The brainstem is an **integral sign**, $$\int$$: a Catmull–Rom spline drawn as a bundle of 21 parallel fibres, slightly thicker where the pons would be. It emerges from under the temporal lobe, just in front of the cerebellum, and curls back where the medulla meets the spinal cord.

## Typography

The site is set in **IBM Plex**, designed by Mike Abbink at IBM with the type foundry Bold Monday and released in 2017 under the SIL Open Font License. Titles and headings use **IBM Plex Sans Light**, a sans-serif with engineered, slightly technical details; text, dates and tables use **IBM Plex Mono**, its monospaced companion, which gives the information on the site the precise rhythm of a lab notebook or a terminal. Because both come from one family, they share proportions and details and read as a single voice.

The front page uses only two styles, the conference name in Plex Sans and everything else in Plex Mono at one size. Inner pages lay out each section with its heading on the left and its text on the right, numbered like the sections of a paper.

The fonts are served from this site rather than from a third party, so the site works without contacting anyone else.

## Colour

The colours come from **Nord**, a palette created by Sven Greb (Arctic Ice Studio) and released under the MIT License, originally for code editors and terminals. It has four groups:

- **Snow Storm** (`#ECEFF4`, `#E5E9F0`, `#D8DEE9`): the paper of the light theme and its hairlines.
- **Polar Night** (`#2E3440` to `#4C566A`): the ink of the light theme, the paper of the dark theme, and the footer.
- **Frost** (`#8FBCBB`, `#88C0D0`, `#81A1C1`, `#5E81AC`): links, the main buttons, section numbers, and the blues of the logo.
- **Aurora** (`#BF616A`, `#D08770`, `#EBCB8B`, `#A3BE8C`, `#B48EAD`): used sparingly, for meaning: the status of each key date (yellow upcoming, green open, red closing soon) and the purple fibres of the logo.

The site follows your device's light or dark setting; the round button at the end of the menu overrides it. The logo is rendered separately for each theme, because it uses the paper colour to hide the fibres that pass behind the cerebrum.

## Built with

The site is built with [Jekyll](https://jekyllrb.com) and hosted on [GitHub Pages](https://pages.github.com); its [source](https://github.com/icmns-org/icmns-org.github.io) is public. It rebuilds every night, so that the status of each key date stays current. The logo is generated by Python with [NumPy](https://numpy.org), [Matplotlib](https://matplotlib.org) and [contourpy](https://contourpy.readthedocs.io), and rasterised with headless Chrome.
