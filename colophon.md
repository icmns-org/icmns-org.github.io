Every curve in the ICMNS logo is computed, not drawn: the folds of the cortex are a solution of a pattern-forming equation, the cerebellum is the phase plane of a model neuron, and the brainstem is an integral sign. The logo is generated by a short Python program with publicly available [source code](https://github.com/icmns-org/icmns-logo).

## The cortex: a Turing pattern

The folds (gyri and sulci) are a labyrinthine Turing pattern, a solution of the **Swift–Hohenberg equation**

$$ \partial_t u = r\,u - \bigl(1 + k^{-2}\nabla^2\bigr)^2 u - u^3 , $$

introduced by Jack Swift and Pierre Hohenberg in 1977 to describe convection rolls, and now a standard model of pattern formation. For $$r > 0$$ the uniform state is unstable to perturbations with wavenumber close to $$k$$, and stripes of wavelength $$2\pi/k$$ emerge.

The logo uses $$r = 0.3$$ and a wavelength of 0.19 (in units where the hemisphere is about 2.3 long). Starting from random noise on a 640 × 480 grid, the equation is integrated for 170 time steps of size 0.5 with a semi-implicit Fourier spectral scheme: the linear operator is treated implicitly in Fourier space and the cubic term explicitly. In the last 60 steps the solution is pinned to a negative value just outside the brain, which rounds off the folds at the edge of the cortex.

The fibres are three **level sets** of the solution, $$u = -0.12$$, $$0.18$$ and $$0.45$$, so each fold is drawn as a bundle of nested curves.

## The colours: tractography

Each short piece of fibre is coloured by its direction, borrowing the idea of the colour code of diffusion tensor imaging (DTI), which maps the orientation of white-matter tracts in the living brain: one colour for front-to-back fibres, one for top-to-bottom, and two for the oblique directions. In the logo the four directions take colours from the Nord palette (below).

## The outline

The outline of the hemisphere is a closed **Catmull–Rom spline** through 22 points traced from a lateral view of a real left hemisphere: frontal lobe, parietal arch, occipital pole, temporal lobe and pole. The program turns it into a **signed distance function**, negative inside and positive outside, which both confines the pattern and draws the two outer contours. The lateral (Sylvian) fissure is cut in with a smooth maximum.

## The cerebellum: a spiking neuron

The cerebellum, a closed spline in proportion to the cerebrum and tucked under the occipital lobe, is filled with the phase plane of the **FitzHugh–Nagumo model**, the classic reduced two-variable model of a spiking neuron, due to Richard FitzHugh (1961) and Jin-ichi Nagumo and colleagues (1962):

$$ \dot v = v - \tfrac{1}{3} v^3 - w + I, \qquad \dot w = \varepsilon\,(v + a - b\,w). $$

Here $$v$$ plays the role of the membrane potential and $$w$$ of a slow recovery variable. With $$a = 0.7$$, $$b = 0.8$$, $$I = 0.5$$ and $$\varepsilon = 0.2$$. Visible in the phase plane are a stable **limit cycle** (in bold), **streamlines** of the vector field: starting points on a jittered grid, each traced a fixed length forwards and backwards along the direction of the flow.

## The brainstem: an integral sign

The brainstem is an **integral sign**, $$\int$$: a Catmull–Rom spline drawn as a bundle of 21 parallel fibres, slightly thicker where the pons would be. It sits just in front of the cerebellum and curls back where the medulla meets the spinal cord; towards the top it passes under the temporal lobe and fades out, as the real brainstem disappears beneath the cerebrum.

## Typography

The site is set in [IBM Plex](https://www.ibm.com/plex/): **IBM Plex Sans** for titles and headings and **IBM Plex Mono** for text. Plex was designed by Mike Abbink at IBM with the type foundry Bold Monday, and is released under the [SIL Open Font License](https://openfontlicense.org). The equations are typeset by XeLaTeX in IBM Plex Mono Italic, with the symbols Plex lacks taken from [Fira Math](https://github.com/firamath/firamath) by Xiangdong Zeng, released under the SIL Open Font License.

## Colour

The colours come from [Nord](https://www.nordtheme.com), a palette by Sven Greb (Arctic Ice Studio), released under the [MIT License](https://github.com/nordtheme/nord/blob/develop/license).

## Built with

The site is built with [Jekyll](https://jekyllrb.com) and hosted on [GitHub Pages](https://pages.github.com); its [source](https://github.com/icmns-org/icmns-org.github.io) is public. The logo is generated by Python with [NumPy](https://numpy.org), [Matplotlib](https://matplotlib.org) and [contourpy](https://contourpy.readthedocs.io). The website and the logo were designed and coded in conversation with [Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/claude), an AI model made by [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com).
